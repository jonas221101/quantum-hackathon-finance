## 1. Where we are, in three sentences

For each investor profile we decide whether the fund's satellite tilt should **keep** NoDur + Hlth + Utils, **switch**
to the proposal HiTec + Hlth + Utils, or go to **review**. Answer: growth switches, risk-sensitive keeps, and balanced
(the assumed default for people who do not choose) goes to review because the answer flips inside the estimation error.
Our quantum part is a rule-preserving QAOA that can only produce valid 3-of-6 portfolios; it is graded against the exact
answer for all 20 portfolios, and we claim no quantum advantage.

| Profile | Verdict | Main evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | **Switch** to HiTec + Hlth + Utils | same winner in all 3 scenario sets; beats current in 89 % of 500 resamples |
| Balanced (default) | **Review**, hold for now | winner flips under the supplied return stress; proposal better in 74 % of resamples |
| Risk-sensitive | **Keep** NoDur + Hlth + Utils | winner in all 3 sets; passes the loss rule in 76 % of resamples |

## 2. The three draft plots for the slides

### 2a. Success rate, quantum vs quantum (`fig_success_rate.png`)

Rule-preserving QAOA (Dicke start + XY mixer, ours) against the organiser's penalty QAOA, plus a penalty QAOA with a
tuned penalty. Ideal simulation, 3 layers, 10 seeds, 4096 shots per run.

| Profile | Valid shots (exactly 3 of 6): ours / organiser / tuned | P(exact best portfolio), median: ours / organiser / tuned |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | 100 % / 98 % / 50 % | 0.23 / 0.05 / 0.08 |
| Balanced | 100 % / 98 % / 55 % | 0.04 / 0.06 / 0.15 |
| Risk-sensitive | 100 % / 97 % / 32 % | **0.78** / 0.05 / 0.07 |

Random guessing among the 20 valid portfolios gives 0.05.

What we can honestly say:

* **100 % valid is guaranteed by construction**, not by tuning: every measured shot of our circuit is a 3-of-6 portfolio.
* The penalty versions face a trade-off. With the organiser's large penalty almost all shots are valid, but the chance of
  the best portfolio stays at guessing level (the penalty drowns the financial signal). With a smaller, tuned penalty the
  signal comes back, but only about a third to a half of the shots are valid. Ours avoids the trade-off.
* The big win is the risk-sensitive profile (0.78 vs 0.05). Growth is better but unsteady between seeds (0.05 to 0.51).
  **Balanced is a near-tie and ours is not better there** with the mean objective (0.04, below guessing in 6 of 10 seeds;
  the CVaR variant reaches 0.25 and decodes the right answer in all 10 runs).
* With shot noise during optimisation (hardware-like), ours drops to 0.36 (risk-sensitive) and 0.02 (growth).

Suggested slide message: "Rules are part of the circuit, so the quantum search can never propose an illegal portfolio.
Where the decision is clear, it also finds the best one far more often. Where the decision is a near-tie, it struggles,
and so does every other method."

### 2b. Sensitivity and break-even for the balanced profile (`fig_estimation_error.png`, plus `fig_decision_map.png`)

* The proposal scores **12 bp per month** better than the current tilt on the 2015-19 estimates.
* That lead disappears if HiTec's expected monthly return is **0.37 points lower**. The estimate itself (1.51 % per month)
  has a standard error of **0.58 points**. The break-even sits at **0.63 standard errors**: well inside the noise.
* The supplied return stress (-0.5 points, 0.86 standard errors) is beyond the break-even, so the winner flips.
* Bootstrap (500 resamples of the 60 months): the proposal is better in **74 %** of resamples; 5-95 % range of its lead
  **-18 to +37 bp per month**. That clears the 70 % gate only barely, and the stress flip already forces review.
* Other break-evens for balanced: switching cost 0.0057 (base 0.002); risk weight 7.5 and 14.9 (base 5).
* For comparison: growth flips only at -0.60 points (1.04 standard errors, beyond the supplied stress); risk-sensitive
  would need HiTec to be 0.55 points *better*.

Suggested slide message: "For the default profile the data cannot tell the two portfolios apart. The honest answer is
review, plus the one belief the committee must state: does tech keep its edge?"

### 2c. Stress-test validation of the loss limit, 2020-24 (`fig_loss_limits.png`, `fig_loss_timeline.png`)

Binding rule: a monthly loss above the threshold (8 % / 5 % / 3 %) may happen in at most 10 % of months (6 of 60).
Fixed one-third weights, the rule's own definition. 2020-24 was never used for a decision.

| Profile | Portfolio | 2015-19 base | Return stress | Covariance stress | **Realised 2020-24** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Growth (8 %) | current / proposal | 1 / 0 | 1 / 0 | 1 / 1 | **2 / 2** (pass) |
| Balanced (5 %) | current / proposal | 2 / 3 | 2 / 3 | 3 / 4 | **5 / 8** (proposal fails) |
| Risk-sensitive (3 %) | current / proposal | 5 / 6 | 5 / 7 | 5 / 9 | **12 / 12** (both fail) |

(Breach months out of 60.)

* For risk-sensitive both portfolios breached the 3 % limit in **20 % of months**, twice the tolerance. The 60-month
  scenario table understated tail risk (COVID crash, 2022).
* For balanced, the proposal would have broken the rule (8 of 60), the current tilt would not (5 of 60). The review
  verdict was the safer call.
* Hindsight wealth (after switching cost): proposal 1.86 vs current 1.38. Drawdown -21 % vs -17 %. One path only; it
  does not prove the 2019 decision right or wrong.

Suggested slide message: "The model's own loss rule held for growth, but not for the cautious profile. Before a real fund
relies on it, the tail-risk model needs more than 60 past months."

## 4. Our view on the synthetic-data option

**Recommendation: we do not need it, and we should say so.** All three plots above run on real data in one
reproducible command. Synthetic numbers on a decision slide would hurt us on exactly the criteria we are graded on
(traceability, reproducible GitHub code, honesty about limits) and contradict our own responsibility story (a
validation team must be able to reproduce every number).

If something is ever needed beyond the six industries, the only defensible use would be a clearly labelled
*synthetic scaling instance* (e.g. 10 to 20 random assets) to show how the circuits grow. It would never feed a verdict.
Bootstrap resamples are not synthetic data: they are the real 60 months redrawn, and we call them that.

Ratio for the deck as it stands: **100 % real data, 0 % synthetic.** Labels we do need: "ideal simulation" vs "with
shot noise", and "decision data 2015-19" vs "hindsight 2020-24".

organiser assumptions; ideal simulation on a laptop; 20 options are solved exactly in milliseconds, so no advantage claim.
